The Premier League's New Cybersecurity Rules

166 requirements. Three deadlines. One rule that most clubs have not noticed.

Starting this season, every Premier League club must meet the League's Information Security Baselines. There are 166 requirements in 22 areas. They come into force in three phases over three years. Each club must send the League a progress report every January and a final report with evidence every April. The rules are on page 693 of a handbook that runs to more than 900 pages. Most clubs have IT vendors who can do the technical work. Few clubs have one person who owns the programme, the evidence, and the reporting to the League.

The League can ask for more information and evidence at any time. A breach is handled under the League's general disciplinary rules. The League Board can fine a club up to £100,000 or refer the case to an independent commission.

The rule most clubs have not noticed
  • Control 22.b (Phase One). Each club must agree an audit plan with a third-party auditor for an independent security assessment each season. This is required from April 2027.
  • Controls 20.a to 20.c (Phase Three). Each club must choose a recognised security framework, get executive approval for it, and have a third party audit the club against it on a regular basis.
Phase One vs. Phase Three
Phase One · 48 controls
Technical controls: EDR, MFA, firewalls, network segmentation, email security, immutable backups, an incident response plan. An IT provider can do most of this work.
Phase Three · 89 controls
Programme management: risk appetite, measuring whether controls work, vendor risk with KPIs, mature security monitoring, a three-year security strategy. An IT provider does not own this work. Someone at the club must.

Promoted clubs must catch up

Under Rule J.14, a club promoted after this season must meet every phase that has already passed, by the next deadline. The Handbook gives this example: a club promoted for 2027/28 must meet Phase One and Phase Two by 30 April 2028.
