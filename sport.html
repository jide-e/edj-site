The gap

The IT work is half the job. The first report is due 10 January.

Phase One looks technical on paper: multi-factor authentication, endpoint protection, firewalls, email security, immutable backups. But the League does not ask whether those controls are installed. It asks the club to assess itself against all 48 requirements by 10 January 2027, file a remediation plan within 28 days if anything falls short, and prove compliance with evidence by 30 April. Installing a control and evidencing it to a regulator are two different jobs. At most clubs, the second one has no owner yet.

Phase Three raises the bar again. Risk appetite, measuring whether controls work, third-party risk with KPIs, security monitoring, a three-year security strategy. That is programme management, and it is more than half the rulebook. An IT provider is not hired to own it, and a club that treats the rulebook as an IT task will find that out in April.

And from Phase One, the rules require an independent third-party security assessment every season (Control 22.b). The audit plan has to be agreed, the evidence has to exist, and someone has to get the club ready before the first deadline, not after it.

What we do

Readiness Assessment

Three to four weeks. We assess the club against the Phase One requirements, identify what can and cannot be evidenced today, and draft the remediation plan the League will ask for if the club is not fully compliant.

Annual Compliance Programme Lead

Ongoing, every season. We run the club's yearly compliance calendar, build the April evidence package, manage the IT provider, the auditor and the insurer, and report to the board in plain language. A UK partner firm carries out hands-on technical work where needed.

What we are not

  • Not your IT provider. You keep yours. We make their work provable to the League.
  • Not your auditor. Control 22.b requires an independent one. We get you ready for them.
  • Not a software vendor. No products, no commissions, no referral fees.

Why EDJ

The Premier League's model is not new. A published list of required controls, a yearly self-assessment, a remediation plan when a company falls short, and evidence on a fixed cycle: United States defence contractors have worked under this model for about a decade, under NIST 800-171 and CMMC.

We have led those programmes. The rulebook is new to football. The job is not new to us.

Promoted clubs

Under Rule J.14, a club promoted into the Premier League must meet every phase whose deadline has already passed, by the next deadline. A club promoted for 2027/28 must meet Phase One and Phase Two by 30 April 2028. If your club is aiming for promotion, the clock has already started.
